BOUNDARIES THAT MATTER

Evidence is not the same as a verdict.

A flagged difference can have a legitimate explanation. Column mapping, duplicate identifiers, partial receipts, split or merged invoice lines, taxes, currency conversion and approved amendments need explicit rules and human validation. A check should only be included in an evaluation when its inputs and expected outcomes can be defined and tested.

05 / SCOPE & TRUST

Questions a careful buyer should ask.

Specific limits are part of a credible reconciliation process.

Is this connected to our ERP or accounting system?

No. The current experience is a browser-side workflow built around CSV exports. There is no ERP write-back or live integration.

Does a flagged variance prove an overpayment?

No. It shows that a configured comparison found a difference in the supplied records. A reviewer must check amendments, units, partial receipts, approvals and source quality before deciding what it means.

How is evaluation success measured?

Against an agreed human-reviewed reference set: confirmed catches, false positives, missed cases, unsupported checks and unresolved edge cases. Metrics are reported only for the agreed scope.

What if we only need supplier statement-to-ledger reconciliation?

That is not yet a supported self-serve feature. The current review is focused on invoice, PO and goods-receipt comparisons. A statement-to-ledger module is a separate planned expansion.

How are fees determined?

The fee is negotiated according to record volume, data quality, rules, validation effort and deliverables. Scope, fee, timeline and acceptance criteria are confirmed in writing before kickoff.
