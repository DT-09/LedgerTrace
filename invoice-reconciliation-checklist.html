AP controls / Practical guide

Invoice reconciliation checklist for distributor finance teams.

Use this checklist when comparing supplier invoices with purchase orders and goods receipts. The aim is not to label every difference as a loss; it is to create a repeatable review trail that helps your team decide what needs investigation.

Operational guidance, not accounting or legal advice. LedgerTrace's sample data is synthetic; no customer savings or accuracy results are claimed.

01 / Before matching

Prepare comparable records first.

Reconciliation is only as defensible as its source data. Keep the original exports, document the period covered, and make sure identifiers and units can be compared consistently.

01

Keep the source files identifiable

Record export date, reporting period, source system and file owner. Preserve the original files so a reviewer can trace a finding back to the input.

02

Normalize identifiers carefully

Check invoice number, supplier, purchase-order number, item or SKU, quantity, unit price and currency. Normalization should not silently merge different suppliers or documents.

03

Agree the comparison unit

Confirm whether each row is a document line, whether quantities use the same units, and whether unit prices include freight, tax, discounts or other adjustments.

02 / Review the exceptions

Six checks worth making explicit.

Duplicate invoice identifiers

Flag repeated invoice numbers for review against supplier, date, amount and document context. A repeated identifier is a candidate, not proof of a duplicate payment.

Invoice vs. PO price

Compare unit prices only when the item, unit of measure, currency and applicable terms are compatible. Show both values and the rule threshold.

Invoice vs. ordered quantity

Surface invoice quantities above the applicable purchase-order quantity. Check approved amendments and split or merged lines before deciding the result is wrong.

Invoice vs. goods received

Identify missing receipt support or quantities above recorded receipts. Confirm that receipts belong to the same supplier, PO, item and relevant period.

Missing or conflicting references

Make missing PO links, unmatched items and supplier conflicts visible. Do not silently treat ambiguous matches as validated.

Payment and credit context

Before calling anything an overpayment, reconcile invoice-linked payments, credits, currency and allocation. A flagged line amount is not verified loss or recoverable money.

03 / Evidence standard

Every exception should answer four questions.

A

What records were compared?

Show invoice, PO and receipt identifiers plus the source-row references.

B

What exactly differed?

Display the compared values, units and currency, not just a red status or a summary total.

C

Which rule triggered?

Make the comparison rule and any threshold explicit so a reviewer can reproduce the reasoning.

D

What did a human verify?

Record whether the finding is confirmed, explained, a false positive or still unresolved. Do not equate a no-finding result with approval to pay.

04 / Repeatable monthly control

Turn the checklist into a consistent review.

  1. Define the population, date range and source exports.
  2. Run the same documented checks against comparable records.
  3. Prioritize exceptions by business context, not by an unverified aggregate amount alone.
  4. Review source evidence and resolve ambiguous cases manually.
  5. Record false positives, missed cases, decisions and corrective actions.
  6. Compare periods only after confirming the data and rule definitions have not changed.

For a new workflow, test against a human-reviewed reference set. Count both cases correctly flagged and cases missed; a demonstration with seeded examples is not evidence of general accuracy.

05 / Buyer questions

Common questions.

Can spreadsheet reconciliation identify every overpayment?

No single rule set can guarantee that. Complex tax, freight, credits, partial payments, supplier master data and split or merged lines can change the correct answer. Define the supported cases and validate them against reviewed outcomes.

Should a flagged difference be treated as a confirmed loss?

No. A difference is an investigation candidate. A human reviewer must confirm the underlying records, contract terms, payment evidence and any credits before asserting loss or recovery.

What should an invoice reconciliation assessment deliver?

A useful bounded assessment can deliver a source-linked exception worklist, a concise findings report, comparison against an agreed reference set, a false-positive and missed-case summary, known limitations and a review handoff.
